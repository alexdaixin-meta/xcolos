Original game: No Thanks!
Players in the original: 3 to 5

Original rules:
Shuffle cards 3-35 and remove 9 face-down to make a 24-card deck. Each player gets 11 chips, kept hidden in hand. On your turn a card is face-up in the middle with a pool of chips on it. You must either take the card and all chips on it into your tableau, or pay one chip from your hand onto the card and pass clockwise. Taking ends the trick; you then flip the next deck card and decide first. Runs of consecutive numbers in your tableau score only the lowest number of the run. Final score is sum of singletons plus lowest of each run minus remaining chips. Lowest score wins.

A PREVIOUS VARIATION of this game was reviewed and tested. The findings:
- balanced FAILED: Key structural change: the card goes round only once, and if all pay, the STARTER takes card plus pot (N chips). Starter's decision, card c that is not adjacent to anything they hold (points cost c, chips worth 1 point each):
| Starter action | Later outcome | Starter's net score change |
| TAKE now | - | +c |
| PAY | someone later takes | +1 (lost chip) |
| PAY | all pay, forced take | +c - (N-1) |
PAY beats TAKE in every row whenever c > 1, and every card is at least 3. The only exception is a card that extends one of the starter's runs downward (marginal points -1). So starter PAY is strictly dominant on almost every card.

Last-offered player (pot N-1 = 3 with 4 players):
- TAKE costs marginal(c) - 3.
- PAY costs +1.
- They take only if marginal(c) < 4, i.e. only for run-adjacent cards.
The same holds, more strongly, for the middle seats.

Result: almost every card falls to its starter, and each non-starter pays 1 chip.

Chip flow with N=4:
- Each player starts 6 rounds.
- Per 4-round cycle, a player gains +3 as starter and pays -3 as non-starter, net 0.
- Everyone ends near 11 chips.
- The scarcity pressure that drives the original game (chips running out, a pot growing over several laps) is gone.

Thoughtless policy 'always PAY' (TAKE only when at 0 chips, which barely happens):
- It is optimal as starter.
- It is optimal as non-starter on every card except run-connecting ones.
- Against sensible play it ends within a few points (a missed run or two) and its win rate is roughly the base rate set by its card draw.
- Against random play it wins most games, because random players TAKE high cards as starters or non-starters at small pots.
So always-PAY is near-dominant: it does not strictly never lose, but it is practically indistinguishable from good play.

'Always TAKE' loses heavily (it collects many high cards with pot 0). That is a degenerate but losing policy, which is acceptable.

Seats: rotation gives each player 6 (N=4) or 8 (N=3) start rounds, so there is no seat edge.

The game as specified collapses: the main decision is trivial, and the original's push-your-luck tension is removed.
- reasoning_over_luck FAILED: Under the intended good strategy (starter PAYs, others PAY unless a card connects a run), nearly every card goes to the player who happened to be starter when it was flipped. Chips end roughly equal, near 11 each.

Final scores are therefore essentially the sum of each player's 6 (or 8) randomly assigned cards minus run savings:
- expected about 114 each for N=4;
- standard deviation of a 6-card sum from 3-35 is about 23 points.
That swamps the few points a skilled player gains by grabbing run-connecting cards at pot ≤ 3.

Chip-count inference is irrelevant, because chip counts never become scarce. There is little to infer about opponents and little way to act on it.

Estimated share of the result decided by the deal (which cards land on your start rounds): 80% or more. The winner under mutual good play is decided by the shuffle.
- change: Remove the starter's guaranteed profit. If every player PAYs, the card is discarded from the game and the pot carries over as the starting pot on the next round's card (pot accumulates across rounds). Chips then drain about N per round, so the pot grows and players must judge when pot ≥ card cost. This restores the original take-or-pay tension within one lap per round and 4 choice steps.
- change: Add a rule for a pot still unclaimed after round 24: it is discarded. Note that discarded cards are out of the game.
- change: With chip scarcity restored, keep chips private: tracking opponents' chip counts from the public history then matters, because a player at 0 must TAKE and can be forced.
- change: Re-verify numerically with a short simulation: always-PAY must run out of chips and be forced into bad cards against sensible play, and starter PAY must no longer be dominant.
- change: Show that, under the revised rules, a player's card set depends mainly on take timing rather than on which start rounds they held. Card-sum variance from seat assignment should no longer decide most games.
The variation that was reviewed: No Thanks! One Lap: SETUP: Take cards numbered 3 to 35 (33 cards). Shuffle them and remove 9 face-down without anyone seeing them; these are out of the game. The remaining 24 cards form the deck. Each player receives 11 chips. A player's chip count is private: only that player is told it. All actions (who paid, who took which card and how many chips) are announced publicly, so careful players can reconstruct others' chips from the history. Each player has a face-up tableau (the cards they have taken) and a running card-points total starting at 0. Players are numbered 1 to N in clockwise order.

ROUNDS: The game lasts 24 rounds, one card per round.

EACH ROUND r:
1. The starter for this round is player ((r-1) mod N) + 1. The top deck card is revealed face-up to everyone. The pot on it is 0 chips.
2. The card is offered once around the table, starting with the starter and going clockwise, until either someone takes it or every player has been offered it once.
3. When the card is offered to a player, that player chooses TAKE or PAY. A player with 0 chips must choose TAKE.
   - PAY: the player moves 1 chip from their hand onto the card (pot +1), and the card is offered to the next player clockwise.
   - TAKE: the player gains every chip in the pot (chips go to their private hand) and places the card in their tableau. The round ends immediately; later players are not offered the card.
4. If every player chose PAY, the card returns to the starter, who must take it together with the whole pot (N chips).
5. CARD-POINT UPDATE: when a player takes card c, their card-points total changes by (c if they do NOT already hold c-1, otherwise 0) minus (c+1 if they DO already hold c+1, otherwise 0). This keeps card points equal to the sum of the lowest card of each run of consecutive numbers in their tableau. Single cards count as runs of one.
6. The result is announced: the card, who took it, the number of chips taken, and the taker's new tableau.

END: After round 24 the game ends. Each player's final score is card points minus chips remaining in hand.

There is no discussion phase. Winner: The player with the LOWEST final score (card points minus remaining chips) wins. If several players tie for lowest, the one among them with more remaining chips wins. If still tied, the game is a draw among them.
