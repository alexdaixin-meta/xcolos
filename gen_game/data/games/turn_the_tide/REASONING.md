# Reasoning and planning: Turn The Tide: Short Seas (revised)

**Verdict: strong.** Winning clearly takes reasoning: the fixed passes make card tracking decisive, and players who predict rivals' forced escapes and time their high cards against the remaining Tides do best. Planning would matter more if the Tide order were less deducible near the end of each deal, or if decisions had effects that carried across deals beyond the single kept card.

From 3 play(s): 480 choices, 480 with a reason given (about 75.2 words each); results {'seat 4': 1, 'seat 3': 1, 'seat 2': 1}.

## Reasoning: 3 of 3

From deal 2 on, almost every decision rests on exact card tracking through the fixed passes, and players use it to predict who will play high, who will land in second place and whether a card can finish in the top two. Players also read rivals' incentives from their Tide levels and lifelines, and this matches the designer's call to 'predict their cards from the public record and the one hidden card per hand'.

- match 1, turn 52, seat 4: "From the passed hands, only Seat 1 holds a card above 46: the 48 that came from my old hand. The unseen cards can be no higher than 33." (Exact inference of opponents' holdings from the pass history and the set-aside cards.)
- match 1, turn 14, seat 2: "Seat 1 likely takes 7, and I am left highest at 9, so I lose a lifeline anyway." (Weighs each option against a rival's forced play and finds that playing high costs nothing extra.)
- match 2, turn 58, seat 2: "Seat 3 now holds the cards I passed, so their remaining cards are 10, 13, 17, 23, 29, 33 and 35. They will almost certainly play their 35 to escape." (Predicts a rival's exact card and plays 37, just enough to beat it, keeping 39.)
- match 1, turn 120, seat 4: "So 23 is never worse and is usually better." (Full case analysis over every possible opponent card in the final hand to maximise score.)

## Planning: 2 of 3

Players routinely keep sure-winning high cards for later low Tides and count the remaining Tide cards to work out the last hands, and several two-hand plans are made and carried out. Most planning reaches only one or two hands ahead, and plans that depend on what rivals will do are short.

- match 1, turn 110, seat 2: "I'll keep 40, which is also guaranteed highest, to take that 7 and finish lowest." (Keeps a sure winner for a known future Low Tide, then adapts at turn 114 once the last Tides are known.)
- match 1, turn 116, seat 4: "If I get tide 7 now, I play 23 next hand and stay at 7." (A plan with branches for the final hand, carried out at turn 120 (seat 4 played 23).)
- match 2, turn 115, seat 3: "I keep 39 for it. That should take Low Tide 1, or at worst High Tide 11." (Keeps a card for the deduced final Tides and plays it at turn 119, taking Low Tide 1 for the win.)
- match 1, turn 35, seat 3: "Getting rid of 34 now leaves me with 25 and 27." (Spends a high card now to shape the hand for the known final Tides.)

## Luck: 1 of 3

In deal 1 the hidden location of the top cards decided some lifeline losses, and the Tide order is random. Later deals are close to perfect information and every seat meets each original hand once, so choices largely decided results; match 1 was decided by deducible card positions.

- match 1, turn 1, seat 1: "only 46, 47 and 48 beat my card. They could be in other hands or set aside." (A deal-1 gamble lost to the unseen 48, costing a lifeline through hidden information.)
- match 2, turn 25, seat 1: "If seat 3 holds 46 and plays it, my 41 would come second" (Unseen cards still create genuine uncertainty in deal 1.)

## Weak spots

- match 1, turn 9, seat 1: Seat 1 assumed only seat 3 would play high, but seat 2 at 14 would be left highest if both 15s escaped (seat 2 itself reasoned this). Both played higher than 36, and seat 1 lost a lifeline. Seat 1 should have counted seat 2's incentive.
- match 1, turn 158, seat 2: Seat 2 claimed 27 'gives me the best chance of having the highest card', but seat 3's known 43 and 48 made that impossible, and seat 1's 35 and 37 shut it out of second place. It was sunk whatever it played; the real error was not seeing at turn 154 that, with the last Tides known to be 3 and 6, staying at 12 left it doomed.
