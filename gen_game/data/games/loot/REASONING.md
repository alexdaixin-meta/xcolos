# Reasoning and planning: Loot: Sealed Raids

**Verdict: strong.** Winning rewards reading trump timing, sequencing ships and sizing commits against the remaining Captains, and the best players did reason and plan this way. But a uniform 'sail the 7 with the Admiral' opening flattened the first four rounds, and more public information to reason from (for example, revealing total crew spent) would make deeper inference and multi-turn plans pay off more.

## Reasoning: 2 of 3

In most contested rounds, players weigh the Admiral threat, rival Captains and likely crew left, and they compute exact strength thresholds and score paths to victory. But many choices copy the table's pattern or are forced zero commits, and several plays ignore obvious signals, such as Captains thrown at unused Admirals and crew sent at Admiral-locked 7s.

## Planning: 2 of 3

Players routinely hold the Admiral for the 7, sail the 5 while it is hidden so the known last ship is a 3, and save Captains and crew for named later rounds, and several of these plans were carried out. Plans that rely on coordinating with others were proposed but rarely executed, and crew budgets were tracked only loosely.

- match 2, turn 89, seat 1: "My Admiral protects my 7-gold ship next round even if I commit 0 crew. So spending all 6 crew now gives me the best chance" (Plans around a trump reserved for the next round and spends crew accordingly.)
- match 1, turn 53, seat 1: "If the 5 is left for last, it becomes a known, tempting target. Sailing the 5 now, while its value is still hidden, protects the more valuable ship." (Sequences ships with the later public reveal in mind.)
- match 3, turn 98, seat 2: "My best path to beat seat 4 is to keep my own 3 and take seat 4's last 3, ending at 18 against their 17." (A multi-round crew plan that the player then pursued.)
- match 1, turn 82, seat 2: "I'm saving my crew and Captain for two later rounds. In round 10 I defend my last ship. Seats 1 and 4 still hold Captains for that raid. In round 9 I can help deny Seat 1's last ship." (Assigns saved resources to specific future rounds and threats.)

## Luck: 1 of 3

There is no random element, and outcomes come from simultaneous hidden commits, so results turn on reads rather than dice. A few cheap blind gambles paid off at the margin, notably the 1-crew snipe that produced a shared win.

- match 3, turn 114, seat 2: "I take this 3 only if seat 3 defends with 0 and no one else raids." (A long-shot blind bet that succeeded and decided a share of the win.)

- match 1, turn 21, seat 3: Committed 6 crew against seat 2, who was openly copying seat 1's Admiral-on-7 script. Seat 3 itself noted the Admiral risk, so committing 0 was the better choice.
