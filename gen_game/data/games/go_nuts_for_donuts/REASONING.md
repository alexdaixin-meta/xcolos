# Reasoning and planning: Go Nuts for Donuts: Secret Budget

**Verdict: strong.** Winning rewarded reading announcements against past behaviour, tracking rivals' used picks, and saving picks plus trust for a late deviation, so reasoning and planning matter at a moderate level. Players fell into a rotation that decided most rounds mechanically; making splits harder to deduce, or making mid-game deviation more rewarding, would push more real inference throughout.

From 3 play(s): 252 choices, 181 with a reason given (about 30.2 words each); results {'seat 1': 2, 'seat 4': 1}.

## Reasoning: 2 of 3

Most choices weigh the announced plans against likely collisions, and endgame decisions work through scores, tiebreaks and rivals' remaining picks. Inferences about hidden splits were sometimes wrong, and in match 3 players repeatedly reasoned the same way and herded onto the same 'open' donut.

- match 2, turn 81, seat 1: "If seat 4 takes Jelly as announced, we clash and neither of us scores. If seats 2 and 3 then clash on Glazed, I win at 35." (Weighs the option against rivals' announced moves and the final scores to find a blocking win.)
- match 3, turn 84, seat 4: "Seat 2 has claimed Glazed for three rounds but actually took Sprinkle, then Cruller, then Cruller. So seat 2 will probably take Cruller again. If I take Cruller too, seat 2 gets nothing, and I win on donuts." (Reads a rival's past behaviour against its words and uses the donut tiebreak correctly. This won the match.)
- match 2, turn 68, seat 4: "Seat 3's past picks show it can win at most one more Glazed or Jelly. That limits its late threat." (Infers a rival's remaining picks from its history, as the designer intended.)
- match 1, turn 82, seat 2: "Seats 3 and 4 both said they will take Glazed, so a collision is very likely. That would leave me stuck at 48." (Compares the odds of each option given what the other seats announced.)

## Planning: 2 of 3

Players often kept premium picks and cooperated in the middle of the game so they could deviate late, and some set up round-10 tiebreak scenarios several turns ahead. These plans were mostly 'cooperate now, defect later' and rarely relied on coordinating specific actions with other players.

- match 1, turn 67, seat 3: "Taking Sprinkle leaves all four of us tied at 42. It keeps the others trusting the rotation and saves both my premium picks for the last two rounds." (Keeps resources and trust for a planned late deviation.)
- match 2, turn 73, seat 1: "A clean Glazed would give me 44 with 9 donuts. If seat 4 takes Jelly and also reaches 44, it would have only 8 donuts, so I would win the tie on donut count." (Plans two rounds ahead, including how the tiebreak would fall.)
- match 2, turn 44, seat 4: "I'll keep cooperating and plan to break from it in a later round, ideally by contesting seat 1's Glazed turn." (Sets a multi-turn plan and later carries it out (turn 68).)

## Luck: 1 of 3

The rules contain no random elements. Simultaneous blind choices, however, made some results hinge on near coin-flip guesses about who would back off, especially in match 3.

- match 3, turn 20, seat 4: "That leaves Jelly most likely open, and it is worth more than Sprinkle." (All four seats made the same guess and collided. Outcomes like this behave close to chance.)

## Weak spots

- match 3, turn 81, seat 1: Claimed seat 3 had used all 6 premium picks, but seat 3 had used only 5 and still had one Glazed. Both took Glazed and collided. A correct count would have shown the collision risk.
- match 2, turn 64, seat 4: Announced the wrong rotation (seat 2 Glazed) and claimed it would finish highest, then defected the next turn. The confused message signalled the defection and gave seat 1 cause to threaten a block.
- match 3, turn 17, seat 1: After a three-way Jelly collision, again picked Jelly as the 'open' option without asking whether the others would reason the same way. All four collided. The uncontested Glazed or Sprinkle was the better-reasoned choice.
- match 2, turn 36, seat 4: Inferred that seat 2 had few or no Glazed picks from one skipped Glazed turn, but seat 2 still had 3. The move worked only because seat 2 backed down.
