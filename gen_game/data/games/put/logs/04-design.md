Original rules:
Use 52 cards ranking 3-2-A-K-Q-J-10-9-8-7-6-5-4, no trumps. Players ante to game score, 5 or 7 points to win match. Deal 3 cards each singly. Non-dealer leads. Any card may be played, no need to follow. Higher rank wins trick; equal ranks tie the trick and it belongs to none. Winner leads next, same leader after a tie. Winning 2 of 3 tricks scores 1 point, trick-and-tie scores none. Before a trick, holder may say "Put" to play for the whole game. If opponent folds, putter scores 1. If opponent says "I see it", the hand is played and winner of most tricks wins the whole game (5 or 7) not just 1. A player may also fold on seeing cards, conceding 1. Deal alternates.

A PREVIOUS VARIATION of this game was reviewed and tested. The findings:
- reasoning_over_luck FAILED: WHAT WAS LOST. The original game's skill lay in sequential trick play. A player leads, sees the reply, decides whether to sacrifice a low card, and can Put mid-hand after seeing tricks. The variation replaces this with one blind simultaneous ordering, made before any of the opponent's cards are seen.

THE ORDERING SUBGAME. Each player knows only their own 3 cards, so ordering is a 6x6 guessing game against unknown cards. Its equilibrium is mixed. For most hands the trick result therefore depends on the deal plus a near-coin-flip guess.

DOMINATED HANDS. When one hand beats the other card by card after sorting, which happens in a large share of deals, ordering does not matter at all and the deal decides the hand outright.

WHAT INFERENCE REMAINS. The only real inference left is the Put/See/Fold signal, which is one bluff-or-call decision per hand.

SENSIBLE PLAY BY BOTH. Take sensible play to mean: Put strong hands, See medium-strong ones, and order at random. The hand result is then set by card strength plus ordering luck. Over 12 hands with stakes of 1 or 3, the variance in deal quality is large compared with the small expected edge from better Put/See decisions.

ESTIMATE. About 65-75% of match outcomes are decided by the deal and blind ordering. A player who reasons well is only weakly favoured, and chance alone often decides the winner.
- change: Restore information during play so that reasoning matters. Play the 3 tricks one at a time as simultaneous single-card polls, and reveal both cards after each trick, so later tricks are planned from what has been revealed.
- change: To stay within 4 choice steps, merge the calls into ONE simultaneous poll before trick 1. Each player picks Pass-and-Fold-to-Put, Pass-and-See, or Put. Resolve the poll arithmetically: Put vs Fold gives the putter +1 and the hand ends; Put vs See or Put vs Put gives stake 3; any other pair of Pass answers gives stake 1. The 3 trick polls then make 4 choice steps.
- change: Lengthen the match to 24-30 hands (within the 40 limit) so that deal variance averages out and better Put/See and trick decisions decide the winner.
- change: Optionally break ties in total points by the number of hands won, to reduce the frequency of drawn matches.

6. Score the hand. The player who won more tricks than the opponent wins the hand and scores the stake (1 point, or 3 points if a Put was seen). If both won the same number of tricks, nobody scores.
7. Announce. Announce the result, both players' cards if they were played, and the scores. The Opener role passes to the other player.
