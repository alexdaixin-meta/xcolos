# Reasoning and planning: Forehead Poker Match

**Verdict: adequate.** Winning needs real reasoning about your own hidden card, mainly odds from the visible cards plus reading talk and bets, and some endgame planning, but card luck decides many pots. The game would demand more if bets came in sequence or there were several betting rounds, so that bet sizing and reading reactions mattered more than the nearly universal Bet 1 and free call.

From 3 play(s): 479 choices, 392 with a reason given (about 39.5 words each); results {'seat 3': 1, 'seat 2': 1, 'seats 2 and 3': 1}.

## Reasoning: 2 of 3

Most betting decisions rest on correct counts of how many unseen cards beat the best visible card. Several players also read hidden information from the lying patterns in talk and from opponents' bet sizes. But nearly every call-step decision was free and got no thought, and some players took opponents' talk at face value, which the designer warned against.

- match 1, turn 86, seat 1: "When my card was high (King or Ace), they called it low. When it was low, they mostly called it high. This hand all three say I hold a King or Queen, so my card is probably low." (Uses the record of past lies, checked at each reveal, to invert the talk and infer their own hidden card.)
- match 2, turn 42, seat 4: "Seat 2 can see my card and still bet 3, so my card is probably low. Folding is better." (Reads their own card from an opponent's bet size and combines it with pot odds.)
- match 1, turn 27, seat 3: "Every card left for me is at least the 2 of Spades, so I win any showdown for certain." (Exact inference from the visible cards that their card cannot lose.)
- match 2, turn 80, seat 4: "each player tells whoever holds the best card they can see that it is low. Seats 1 and 3 both aimed that at seat 2 (10 of Clubs). That suggests my card is below the 10 of Clubs" (Uses the talk pattern to place their own card relative to the visible ones.)

## Planning: 2 of 3

Inside a hand, players often bet 1 as a cheap probe and kept the call step as a later exit. Players near the end worked out chip margins to lock a win or find the only route to a tie. Across most of the match there was little multi-hand setup beyond 'I'm behind, take risk', and talk aimed at making the leader lose.

- match 1, turn 158, seat 3: "If I fold, the most seat 4 can do is win my ante plus the antes and maximum bets of seats 1 and 2... Folding guarantees the win." (Endgame calculation that secures the match.)
- match 1, turn 159, seat 4: "a draw needs all four seats to bet 3 and call, and my card to beat seat 3's Q of Hearts." (Works out the only remaining route, which depends on what the other players do, and backs it with a message to the table.)
- match 1, turn 8, seat 4: "A 1-chip bet is a cheap probe: if seat 1 bets, I can fold at the call step having lost only 1 more chip." (Plans the bet around the later call/fold option.)
- match 2, turn 150, seat 2: "Seat 1 holds the 5 of Spades, so seat 3 or seat 4 will likely take this pot, which keeps my lead safe. Folding protects the lead." (Gives up positive expected value to protect the lead, planning around rivals' likely results.)

## Luck: 2 of 3

With one card each and mostly minimum bets, who was dealt the strong cards, or saw weak tables, decided many pots. Seat 3's lead in match 1 came largely from repeatedly seeing weak opponents. Choices still mattered at the margins, such as cheap folds against visible Aces and Kings and endgame lead protection.

- match 1, turn 27, seat 3: "Seats 1, 2 and 4 hold the 2 of Clubs, 2 of Diamonds and 2 of Hearts, the three weakest cards in the deck." (The deal handed seat 3 a certain win and the hand's biggest pot (12) so far.)
- match 1, turn 150, seat 4: "My card has roughly an 18% chance (9 of 49 unseen cards) of beating seat 3's Queen of Hearts, so I'll bet the maximum." (A desperation bet that happened to hold the Ace of Hearts, which made everyone else fold.)

## Weak spots

- match 1, turn 5, seat 1: Folded the K of Spades because three players called it low. Everyone telling one player to fold is itself a sign that player is strong; a Bet 1 probe was better.
- match 1, turn 26, seat 2: Bet 3 while seeing an Ace on seat 3, trusting seat 3's own claim that it 'will likely fold'. Seat 3 could see three 2s, so it was sure to bet. Folding, or Bet 1, was clearly better and would have saved 3 chips.
- match 2, turn 100, seat 1: The reasoning argued for 'Bet 2' with a 59% edge while 17 chips behind, but the chosen action was Fold. This threw away a favourable spot.
- match 2, turn 26, seat 4: Under the rules (only one card each, all bets visible), nearly every decision was a minimum Bet 1 followed by a free call. Players rarely used bet size to extract value or pressure opponents, as the designer expected strong play to do.
