Original game: Kobayakawa
Players in the original: 3 to 6

Original rules:
Each player gets 4 kamons and one hidden card from a 1-15 deck; one card is revealed as Kobayakawa. Place 8 kamons in the center pot. Each round has a Draw phase: in turn order each player either draws from deck to keep one of the two cards discarding the other face-up, or blindly replaces the Kobayakawa card. Then Fight phase: in order each player chooses to bet 1 kamon to fight or pass. Only fighters reveal. The fighter with the lowest card adds the Kobayakawa value to his total; highest total wins. Winner takes all bet kamons plus 1 from center. Ties broken by earlier turn order. If sole fighter, wins without revealing. Winner becomes start player. Players with 0 kamons are eliminated. Continue until 2 kamons remain in center; last round costs 2 kamons to fight and winner takes both remaining. Most kamons at end wins.

A PREVIOUS VARIATION of this game was reviewed and tested. The findings:
- playable FAILED: WHAT WORKS:
- Cards: 5 players x 2 cards + K = 11 of 15, so the deck suffices.
- Rules coverage: the zero-fighter, one-fighter, multi-fighter, tie, empty-centre and zero-kamon cases are all covered.
- Finite and arithmetic: the game is a fixed 10 rounds and is decided by kamon counts the game keeps.
- Limits: the stated complexity is within every limit (steps 15/20, choices 2/4, attributes 12/20, hidden 2/3, draws 1/1, rounds 10/40). Finding the lowest and highest among a variable set of fighters needs calc steps, but fits.
- Restrictions: no board, judge or clock is needed. The draw is a single shuffle with remove/append operations.

THE FAILURE: TALK. This is a 3-5 player game, and the design is silent on talk. The original's Fight phase was sequential, so later players could condition on earlier fight or pass choices. Making the choice simultaneous removed that, and nothing replaced it. Whether to fight depends heavily on who else fights:
- a middle card wins with 2 fighters but never with 3;
- the break-even p falls from 1/3 to 1/4 to 1/5 as fighters are added.
Players have opposed interests, since the game is zero-sum on the pot. Bluffing 'I'm fighting with a high card' to deter others costs a believer real kamons, which is exactly where talk belongs. Without it, the fight poll beyond the KEEP/SWAP signal is largely guessing about each other. The repeated-cooperation check is not applicable, because there is no cooperative option.
- change: Playable: add a simultaneous free-text talk step after the discards are announced and before the Fight choice. All players write a public message, all messages are shown, then the simultaneous FIGHT/PASS poll runs. State that talk is not binding. Alternatively, make the Fight choice sequential starting with the previous round's winner, with the start rotating if there is no winner, so later fighters see earlier choices as in the original. If sequential order is used, rotate the start every round to avoid a fixed seat edge. Update steps_per_round (+1 or +2) and keep it at or below 20.
The variation that was reviewed: Kobayakawa Duel Rounds: SETUP: Each player starts with 5 kamons. The centre starts with 10 kamons. The game lasts exactly 10 rounds.

EACH ROUND:
1. Deal. Shuffle a fresh deck of 15 cards numbered 1 to 15. Deal each player one face-down hand card, which only they see. Deal each player one face-down reserve card, which nobody sees. Turn one card face up: this is the Kobayakawa card, value K. Announce K.
2. Draw choice. All players at the same moment choose KEEP or SWAP. A SWAP player discards their hand card face up and takes their reserve card as their new hand card, then looks at it. Reserve cards of KEEP players are not used. Announce every discarded card and who discarded it.
3. Fight choice. All players at the same moment choose FIGHT or PASS. A player with 0 kamons must PASS. Each FIGHT player puts 1 kamon into the pot.
4. Resolve.
   - No fighters: nothing happens.
   - Exactly one fighter: that player takes their own kamon back. Their card is not revealed and the centre pays nothing.
   - Two or more fighters: all fighters reveal their cards. Each fighter's total is their card, except that the fighter with the lowest card adds K to it. The fighter with the highest total wins. If totals tie, the tied fighter with the lower card wins. The winner takes the whole pot. If the centre has at least 1 kamon, the winner also takes 1 kamon from the centre. Losing fighters lose their stake.
5. Announce all fighters, revealed cards, totals, the winner, and every player's kamon count.
6. After round 10 the game ends. Winner: After round 10, the player with the most kamons wins. If two or more players tie for most, the game is a draw.
