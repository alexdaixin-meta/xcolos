# Reasoning and planning: Ys: Brokers of the Blind Market

**Verdict: strong.** Winning takes real reading of opponents' bid habits and promises, plus careful final-round score math; all three matches were decided by under 15 points, through who over- or under-committed in the last round. The market vote collapsed into a dominant 'vote your own color' pattern that revealed every type by round 2, so the bluffing and price-starving the designer intended never happened, and making the vote costly or hidden, or rewarding votes against rivals, would demand more reasoning and planning.

From 3 play(s): 162 choices, 123 with a reason given (about 37.8 words each); results {'seat 2': 1, 'seat 1': 1, 'seat 3': 1}.

## Reasoning: 2 of 3

Allocation decisions routinely infer rivals' secret colors from bids and votes, track opponents' typical bid sizes, and project final scores, often with correct arithmetic. But color inference was trivial after round 1, and about half the decisions are market votes justified only by 'my color, I hold N gems', which is a correct dominant choice rather than an inference.

- match 1, turn 5, seat 2: "Seat 1 says they want Red, probably with about 3 influence. Seats 1 and 3 both named Commerce, so I'll stay out of that fight. Nobody claimed Blue, so 2 there should win it cheaply." (Weighs rivals' stated intentions against its own allocation in the opening round.)
- match 2, turn 49, seat 1: "All-in Green (0/0/6/0) loses: I reach 251, Seat 2 reaches 268." (Works through its own options against each likely opponent play, with correct score projections. This choice won the match.)
- match 2, turn 5, seat 2: "Seat 3 is talking up Blue, but Blue is my secret color, so they are probably bluffing or baiting." (Uses private knowledge to see through a rival's table talk.)
- match 3, turn 54, seat 3: "13 gems × 9 = 117, plus 10 Red × 9 again = 90, plus 22 commerce =" (Exact final-score computation that matches the actual result.)

## Planning: 2 of 3

Players bluff colors early to protect later districts, raise a color's price before holding any of its gems, and base later bids on rivals' promises to stay out of a district. Most plans span only one or two rounds, though, and the late-game coalitions against the leader were mostly one-round reactions.

- match 3, turn 9, seat 3: "Raising Red sets up value for the Red gems I plan to collect." (Votes to build future value before holding any gems of that color.)
- match 3, turn 6, seat 3: "Skipping Red this round also helps hide my favored color." (Gives up gems now to keep its type hidden for later rounds.)
- match 2, turn 41, seat 2: "Seat 3 has promised to stay out of Blue. So a single point in Blue should win it outright... Four in Commerce beats Seat 3's usual 3" (Builds a plan on a rival's earlier promise and on that rival's bid habits, then breaks its own pledge on Commerce to profit.)
- match 3, turn 51, seat 3: "If I win Red first (+36) and Commerce first (+8), I can pass them even if they win Green." (Carries out a final-round plan after publicly claiming it would go all-in on Green. The leader overcommitted to Green and Seat 3 won.)

## Luck: 0 of 3

The only chance element is the deal of secret colors, and the types are symmetric, so the deal favored no seat. Results were decided by simultaneous-move guessing and allocation choices, which is strategic uncertainty rather than chance.

- match 1, turn 53, seat 2: "prices become Red 9, Blue 9, Green 9" (Final prices were identical in all three matches, so the deal of colors had no differential effect.)

## Weak spots

- match 1, turn 42, seat 3: Put all 6 into Blue although Seat 2 had bid only 1 there in recent rounds, leaving Commerce stuck at 3. Something like 0/4/1/1 would still have won Blue and picked up points elsewhere. Seat 3 lost by 5.
- match 3, turn 49, seat 1: Went 0/0/6/0 trusting Seat 3's claim to go all-in on Green, although Seat 3 had held Red all game. It also miscounted: 'even if each of them takes their own color' ignored Commerce. 0/0/5/1 still wins Green and takes second in Commerce (+3), giving 230 to 229 and the match.
- match 2, turn 32, seat 2: Kept its promise to raid Green with 3 and put only 1 in Commerce. That won a single Green gem and handed Seat 3 eight Commerce points, as Seat 1 then pointed out. Defending Blue and contesting Commerce was the better option.
- match 1, turn 24, seat 3: Abandoned Commerce from round 3 on to over-guard Blue with 5 against raids that never came. It finished with 3 commerce and lost a close race.
