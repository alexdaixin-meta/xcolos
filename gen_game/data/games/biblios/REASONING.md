# Reasoning and planning: Biblios: Scriptorium Lots (revised)

**Verdict: adequate.** Winning rewards card counting against the public deck makeup, reading rivals' purses and keeping hidden majorities, so reasoning and multi-round planning matter. The draw and frequent tied-bid discards still swing results. Binding or contested talk, and a reason to bluff or neutralise Churches, would push play toward the opponent modelling the designer intended.

From 3 play(s): 44 choices, 44 with a reason given (about 57.1 words each); results {'seat 3': 2, 'seat 1': 1}.

## Reasoning: 2 of 3

Most splits weigh which card to keep, give or auction against public records, discards and rivals' purses. Late-game decisions often use exact card counting, but early choices are generic ('weakest card goes to seat 2') and a few rest on wrong premises. Nobody reasons about bluffing in talk or about neutralising a Church, two things the designer stressed.

- match 3, turn 81, seat 3: "By elimination, the seven cards nobody has seen are Red 3, Blue 4, Green 3, Green 4, Yellow 3, Purple 2 and Purple 4. Seats 1 and 2 kept them." (Exact deduction of hidden kept cards from the public deck makeup, used to choose a keep that ties-proofs Yellow.)
- match 2, turn 87, seat 3: "The only blue card not yet seen is Blue 4, so the best any opponent can reach is 6. Raising blue is a guaranteed extra point for me." (Church action chosen from a correct count of the remaining cards rather than a vague preference.)
- match 1, turn 73, seat 2: "Giving Gold 2 to seat 3 would raise them to 5 gold, more than my 4. They could then outbid me in this auction and the final one." (Weighs rivals' purses and later auctions when deciding what to give.)
- match 2, turn 8, seat 2: "Giving Blue 1 to seat 3 hands them nothing in a colour I'm already building, unlike Yellow." (Weighs the gift against the colour races, though only in general terms.)

## Planning: 2 of 3

Players build hidden majorities over several rounds, place cards at auction so they can bid on them, and save gold or Church actions for raising a colour once it is locked. No plan relies on predicting a specific rival's response beyond purse limits.

- match 1, turn 30, seat 3: "I'm keeping the Church so I get a church action this round, and I'll use it to raise Purple, where my hidden Purple 4 already leads." (Pairs an earlier hidden keep with a later die raise.)
- match 3, turn 67, seat 1: "Keeping Red 3 puts my Red at 5. If I also win Red 1 at auction, my Red reaches 6, which beats the most anyone else could reach in Red." (Two-step plan, keep then auction win, to secure a colour; it was carried out.)
- match 1, turn 73, seat 2: "I keep my 4 gold, the most at the table, for round 12." (Saves gold for the final auction.)

## Luck: 2 of 3

The draw decides which high cards reach each active player; seat 1 in match 3 was dealt Blue 4, Purple 4 and Red 3 as its own keeps. Frequent tied bids discarded many lots, so what got discarded shaped colour races as much as the choices did.

- match 3, turn 52, seat 1: "I keep Purple 4. With my kept Purple 2, that gives me Purple 6, a strong lead in a colour nobody is chasing." (A key majority came from drawing the top card on one's own turn.)
- match 1, turn 23, seat 2: "Yellow 4 is already discarded and Yellow 3 is now the best Yellow card left." (A tied-bid discard reshaped the Yellow race.)

## Weak spots

- match 1, turn 66, seat 1: Seat 1 auctioned Blue 3 and gave Blue 1 to seat 2, reasoning that seat 2 'only pass[es] my 5 if they actually win the Blue 3 auction'. Seat 1 had 2 gold against seat 2's 8, so seat 2 would almost surely win it, and it did, taking Blue. Keeping Blue 3 would have locked Blue at 8.
- match 1, turn 8, seat 2: Seat 2 gave the Church to seat 3 as 'helps them least', but seat 3 used it to lower Blue, the one colour seat 2 publicly led. Giving Purple 3 and auctioning the Church was the better option.
- match 3, turn 30, seat 3: Seat 3 kept Gold 4, citing 'gold tiebreaks', which do not exist in the rules, and gave Red 2 to seat 1. Seat 1 later won Red. Keeping a Red 2 instead would have contested that colour.
- match 3, turn 51, seat 3: Seat 3 claimed 'I also hold Green 4', but its Green came from Green 2, 1 and 1. This misreads its own position.
