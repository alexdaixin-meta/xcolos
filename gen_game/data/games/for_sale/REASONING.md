# Reasoning and planning: For Sale: Sealed Bids

**Verdict: adequate.** Winning rewards reasoning in the selling phase, where public hands allow precise inference and multi-round sequencing. The buying phase collapsed into a near-universal bid of 1 decided by tie priority, so little of the designer's intended coin-reading took place. More informative signals, such as revealing the winning bid, or a rule that makes minimal bids costly, would push players to infer coin totals and plan their spending.

From 3 play(s): 135 choices, 135 with a reason given (about 60.9 words each); results {'seat 3': 1, 'seat 2': 1, 'seat 1': 1}.

## Reasoning: 2 of 3

Selling decisions usually rest on specific, correct reads of the public hands: which card is unbeatable, the cheapest card that still secures a rank, and expected values from the unseen cheques. Buying decisions mostly repeat the same 'a bid of 1 costs nothing unless first' rule, with only occasional inference about rivals' coins, and one of those inferences was clearly wrong.

- match 1, turn 33, seat 3: "21 beats every other card they hold, including seat 1's 20. Playing 22 or 24 would win exactly the same cheques as 21" (Picks the minimum card that secures the target rank, based on the opponents' known hands.)
- match 1, turn 8, seat 2: "My bid of 1 won with tie priority, so they bid 1 or less. This round I have the lowest tie priority, so I need to bid strictly above them." (Infers rivals' hidden bids from public outcomes and the tie-break order.)
- match 3, turn 45, seat 3: "The expected highest of the three is about 10.2 and the expected lowest is about 2.1." (Uses the remaining cheque pool to compare the expected value of two sequencing options.)
- match 2, turn 14, seat 2: "Seat 1 has probably spent heavily winning HIGH three times, and I have the most coins" (Reads a rival's coin drain from public wins, as the designer intended.)

## Planning: 2 of 3

Players routinely keep their strong properties for rounds with wide cheque spreads and dump weak cards on flat rounds. A few explicit two-round plans account for what rivals will do. Buying-phase play is almost entirely move-by-move.

- match 2, turn 44, seat 2: "So I want my 20 in the round where they play 16, and my 13 in the round where they play 12." (A two-round plan that depends on predicting the rival's sequencing.)
- match 2, turn 38, seat 2: "I'll dump my lowest card, 9, and keep 23, which beats everything left once 25 is gone." (Gives up the current round to set up a guaranteed win once the rival's top card is spent.)
- match 3, turn 43, seat 1: "If I play 18, I get the 7 now. Then my 20 beats their 19 and seat 3's 14 next round" (Plans across the last two rounds against the likely plays of both opponents.)

## Luck: 2 of 3

The property and cheque draws set much of each hand's value. Many buying rounds were settled by tie-break priority among identical bids of 1, so seat order decided who got HIGH. Cheque spreads also decided whether a dumped card scored well, but skilled sequencing still moved the margins.

- match 2, turn 24, seat 3: "a bid of 1 costs nothing unless I come first, and then I pay just 1 for the 30" (All three seats bid 1 and priority handed seat 2 the 30, the deck's best card.)
- match 1, turn 27, seat 3: "my 9 may still finish in the middle and take the 9 cheque" (Seat 3 got the 13 cheque with a 9 because of a lucky cheque mix and rivals dumping low.)

## Weak spots

- match 2, turn 22, seat 1: Claimed seat 2 'likely has 10 or more coins', although seat 2 had just paid full price to win HIGH (20) the round before. With about 8 coins and rivals likely to bid 1, a bid of 2 would probably have taken the 30 cheaply. Seat 1 bid 1 and took the 15.
- match 1, turn 13, seat 1: Bid 3 aiming only for MIDDLE (18) in a round with a 26 on offer, despite having coins to spare. Seat 2 took the 26 for 6. A slightly higher bid had a real chance at the top card.
- match 1, turn 15, seat 3: Claimed 'I have 6 coins left' when seat 3 should have had about 9 (18-2-0-4-6=6 if the 6 bid paid in full, so the count may be right, but it then hoarded to the end and finished with 5 coins). Minor; reasoning was mostly coherent.
