# Reasoning and planning: Three Pigs: Secret Materials

**Verdict: strong.** Winning rewarded inferring types and stockpiles and timing the leftover tiebreak, with a modest amount of two-round planning, though play converged on cooperative splits and Wolves were never used. More forward planning would be needed if Wolves had a real cost or gain, or if the tiebreak rewarded more than raw leftovers, so that a stall on the leader is actually worth sending.

From 3 play(s): 172 choices, 146 with a reason given (about 33.3 words each); results {'seats 1 and 3': 1, 'seat 2': 1, 'seat 3': 1}.

## Reasoning: 2 of 3

Players regularly worked out rivals' Favoured Materials from build costs and gathering, and in the end rounds they reasoned about the leftover-resource tiebreak and about bluffs. Some decisions were wrong or did not match the stated reasoning: a stated Wolf that was never sent, a collision caused by misreading a rival, and a pass justified by a 'next round' that would not come.

- match 1, turn 15, seat 1: "Seat 3 built two Wood sections from 3 Wood, so Wood is probably their favoured material. That likely makes Seat 2's favoured material Straw" (Infers one rival's type from build cost and uses the 3-player permutation to deduce the other.)
- match 3, turn 13, seat 3: "Seat 2 built two Wood sections from only 2 Wood, so they likely favour Wood. Seat 1 paid 2 for one Wood section, so they likely favour Brick." (Correct hidden-type inference from public build counts against known gains.)
- match 1, turn 52, seat 2: "Seat 1 looks Brick-favoured with about 3 Brick and 2 Wood. Seat 3 looks Wood-favoured with about 4 Straw and 1 Brick. If we all finish, the most leftover resources wins, and I start a point behind." (Reconstructs rivals' private stockpiles to judge the tiebreak.)
- match 3, turn 50, seat 2: "Seat 3 also needs Straw, so their wolf threat may be a bluff to scare me off." (Correctly reads a rival's announced Wolf as a self-serving bluff.)

## Planning: 2 of 3

Players often planned two rounds ahead: they kept stock to finish next round, chose gather spots for denial or leftovers once their builds were covered, and counted on the unblockable Market. Plans that depended on manipulating rivals' moves were attempted through talk but rarely went beyond a single round.

- match 3, turn 38, seat 1: "Next round, Market gives me 1 Brick, which nobody can block. That covers my Brick walls and roof." (Two-round finishing plan built on a guaranteed resource.)
- match 2, turn 41, seat 2: "That 1 Wood pays for the Roof next round, which would be my third completed house." (Sequences builds so the next round finishes the game and keeps Straw as leftover.)
- match 3, turn 40, seat 3: "I don't need to gather to finish... So this round is about two things: blocking Seat 2 and banking leftovers for a possible tiebreak." (Uses a gather that is not needed for building to deny a rival and prepare for the tiebreak.)

## Luck: 1 of 3

Bonus draws shifted pile sizes, but every match was decided by simultaneous-choice collisions and the leftover tiebreak, both driven by player decisions. The deal mattered little because the types are symmetric.

- match 1, turn 53, seat 3: "So I should gather the biggest pile. Brickyard has 5 and nobody has claimed it firmly." (The final result came from all three players choosing the same pile, a decision collision rather than chance.)

## Weak spots

- match 2, turn 51, seat 3: Reasoned that 'sending the Wolf to Forest is the only way to stop them' but chose plain Forest. A Wolf was useless anyway, since Seat 2 already held the 1 Wood. A better move was Fields to contest Seat 2's tiebreak leftovers, or Brickyard to cut Seat 1.
- match 2, turn 28, seat 3: As the Straw-favoured player, left uncontested Fields because Seat 2 claimed it, although Seat 2 had been inferred as Wood-favoured. Seat 3 collided at Forest and wasted the round.
- match 1, turn 53, seat 3: Chose the biggest pile for leftovers without expecting rivals to reason the same way. A three-way collision left a draw, when the uncontested 3 Straw or 3 Wood would have won the tiebreak outright.
- match 3, turn 55, seat 1: Passed 'for next round' although seats 2 and 3 were visibly finishing that action, so there was no next round. Seat 1 had itself noted at turn 52 that they would finish this round.
- match 3, turn 35, seat 2: No one ever used a Wolf, even when a leader was one pile from finishing (e.g. Wolf-Fields in match 3 round 5 would have stopped Seat 2). The designer's stalling tool went unused, and talk-only block calls were ignored.
