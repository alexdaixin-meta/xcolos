# Reasoning and planning: Budget English Auctions

**Verdict: adequate.** Winning rewards tracking revealed cards, coins and tie-breaks, and the best final-round plays were exact threshold calculations, but much mid-game bidding was simple value-margin play, and deck order heavily shaped who could strike late. More bidding steps, hidden coin totals, or a reason to keep reciprocal deals made in talk would demand deeper inference and multi-turn plans.

From 3 play(s): 200 choices, 133 with a reason given (about 26.8 words each); results {'seat 3': 1, 'seat 4': 2}.

## Reasoning: 2 of 3

Players regularly weighed bids against the tie-break rules, the claims at the table and exact score thresholds, and several counted which high cards rivals had already revealed. Many middle-game bids, though, were simple 'value minus margin' choices that ignored opponents' remaining decks, and at least one inference about a rival's card was wrong.

- match 2, turn 72, seat 1: "Seat 2's 14 and 16 are already used, so their best remaining card is 12. Seat 4's 16 is gone too, so their best is 14. Seat 3 could still hold a 16." (Card counting from revealed winner values to infer what rivals can still hold.)
- match 3, turn 86, seat 4: "seat 1's 16 was already revealed in round 5. Seat 2's 16 was used in round 1. If anyone ties me at 6, I still win on coins. A bid of 16 locks in the win." (Combines card counting, the coin tie-break and score thresholds into a winning blocking bid.)
- match 1, turn 84, seat 2: "a tie at 6 loses to seat 3, who has more coins. My score would be 4 + 10 − price, so I can pay at most 7." (Exact calculation of the highest bid that can still win the game.)
- match 1, turn 12, seat 1: "This is the last bidding step. Seats 2 and 3 have dropped, and seat 4 leads but is not asked, so a raise to 11 cannot be answered." (Uses the step structure to make a raise no one can answer.)

## Planning: 2 of 3

Players often kept coins back for known high cards still in their own deck, and late-game spending was timed against which cards remained. Plans that depend on what opponents will do over several turns were rare; the talk-based 'make the leader pay' coordination was only declared, never set up as a reciprocal plan.

- match 1, turn 37, seat 2: "my better cards (10, 12, 14) are still to come, so I want to save coins." (Keeps budget for known future high cards.)
- match 1, turn 43, seat 1: "My remaining cards are low (4, 6, 8, 10), so spending coins now costs me little." (Spends coins now because the rest of the deck will not need them.)
- match 3, turn 66, seat 4: "I would still have 26 coins, enough to bid near value on my 12 and 16 cards in the last two rounds." (Checks a denial bid against the coins needed for the last two rounds.)
- match 2, turn 61, seat 1: "If I win at 6, I gain 4 and keep 19 coins for the 16." (Sizes the current bid to keep budget for a later card.)

## Luck: 2 of 3

Shuffled deck order decided who held the 16 or 14 in the late rounds when rivals were low on coins and cards, and in matches 1 and 3 the eventual winner held the 16 in round 8. Choices still mattered, since thresholds and denial bids decided the close finishes.

- match 1, turn 74, seat 3: "With a 10 this round and a 16 still to come in round 8, I'll open at 6" (The winner's 16 arrived in the final round, when no one could contest it.)
- match 3, turn 59, seat 4: "I lead with 7, with 12 and 16 still to come." (The eventual winner's late high cards came from deck order.)

## Weak spots

- match 3, turn 66, seat 4: Inferred that seat 2's raise to 5 meant a card of 8 or more and overpaid 7 on a 6. Seat 2 actually held a 4 and was only denying, so letting seat 2 win at 5 for -1 was the better-reasoned option.
- match 1, turn 20, seat 4: Held a 14 but opened at only 6 and then dropped, letting seat 2 take a 16 for 12. A step-2 raise to 13 would have kept a small profit or forced seat 2 to pay more.
- match 1, turn 65, seat 1: Raised to break-even on a 10 and was left with 6 coins, which made them nearly irrelevant in rounds 7 and 8. The gain from denying seat 2 was small next to the lost flexibility.
