# Reasoning and planning: Lost Cities: Wager Draft

**Verdict: adequate.** Winning rewards card counting, burial avoidance, a sensible Wager and timely denial, but who holds priority when the high cards appear often decides the result, and inference about the rival's Wager rarely changed a choice. More rounds after the Wager, or rows that force a real trade-off between denial and building, would make reasoning and planning matter more.

From 3 play(s): 78 choices, 78 with a reason given (about 61.9 words each); results {'seat 1': 1, 'seat 2': 2}.

## Reasoning: 2 of 3

Players tracked which cards were still unseen, worked out exact score swings, and often checked whether a card could be denied to the rival. Inference about the rival's hidden Wager was rare but correct when it happened, and many decisions were simple burial avoidance that needed little thought.

- match 2, turn 25, seat 2: "Seat 1's best possible total is 18 (if they wagered Blue), so 20 wins." (Correct worst-case bound on the rival's total, with their Wager unknown, before choosing the last card.)

- match 2, turn 14, seat 1: "the only Blue cards left to draw are 3, 5, 7 and 9... the 5 only helps if it shows up before the 7 or 9, which is unlikely." (Counts the remaining cards and reasons about the order they are likely to appear.)
