# Reasoning and planning: Cash and Guns: Standoff

**Verdict: strong.** Winning takes real reasoning about hidden Bang counts, who will Duck, and exact end-state cash, plus some planning about when to spend Bangs. Play settled into a repetitive 'gang up on the leader, leader ducks' loop that rewards reading the talk more than deep multi-turn plans. Public tie-break rules, or more variable bullet hands, would make inference and timing matter more.

From 3 play(s): 366 choices, 304 with a reason given (about 33.8 words each); results {'seat 1': 1, 'seat 3': 1, 'seat 4': 1}.

## Reasoning: 2 of 3

Many decisions weigh options against the state: players count revealed Clicks to infer remaining Bangs, predict who will Duck under three guns, and work out exact final-round cash outcomes. Still, a large share of decisions are the generic 'nobody is aiming at me, so stand', and some players repeated an invented tie-break rule.

## Planning: 2 of 3

Players routinely keep Bangs for late rounds, load Clicks when they expect to Duck, and plan to dump Bangs unseen by ducking. Some of these plans were carried through to round 8. Plans seldom span several turns in a way that depends on what opponents will do, and several players emptied their Bangs early, so their final-round threats were hollow.

- match 2, turn 75, seat 3: "It is better saved for round 8, when it can stop the leader from taking a share and nobody can hit back afterward." (Keeps a resource for a later turn on purpose.)
- match 2, turn 57, seat 1: "Once I see who is aiming at me, I can Duck to get rid of it unseen, or Stand and fire at seat 2" (Loads a Bang on a cheap pot and keeps the stance decision open for after targets are shown.)
- match 1, turn 22, seat 2: "I plan to load a Click so I don't waste one of my two remaining Bangs on a likely Duck." (Chooses target and bullet together, planning for an expected Duck.)
- match 2, turn 105, seat 1: "Playing the Click now keeps my Bang for round 8. A Bang fired in the last round can't be answered" (A multi-round hold plan that was carried out in round 8.)

## Luck: 1 of 3

Results mostly came from stand/duck reads and gang-ups, but the margins were tiny (24 vs 23.67k in match 1). Random pot sizes and blind guesses about hidden discards settled the last round at the edge.

- match 3, turn 122, seat 4: "Both may have spent their Bangs in earlier unrevealed discards. In that case I share and finish on top." (The winner's final stand rested on an unverifiable guess about hidden cards.)
- match 1, turn 117, seat 1: "Seat 4 has ducked every time it was targeted, so it will probably duck again." (The deciding round turned on a stance prediction, not on a die roll; chance only moved the margin.)
