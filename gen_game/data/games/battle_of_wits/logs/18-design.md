Original game: Battle of Wits
Players in the original: 2 to 2

Original rules:
The game is for two players: the Poisoner and the Chooser. Two identical goblets of wine are placed on the table. In secret, the Poisoner decides which goblet(s) contain poison - in the classic version, exactly one goblet is poisoned, though the story version allows both. The Chooser then studies the opponent and selects one goblet for himself; the remaining goblet goes to the Poisoner. Both players drink simultaneously. If the Chooser drank poison he dies and loses; if he drank the safe goblet, the Poisoner dies and loses. In a variant, the Poisoner may be immune and wins regardless, making the choice purely about deduction and bluffing about who would poison which glass.

A PREVIOUS VARIATION of this game was reviewed and tested. The findings:
- playable FAILED: Rules are otherwise finite (6 fixed rounds), scores arithmetic 0-6, no judge/board/clock needed. Complexity claims within limits: steps_per_round 4 <=12, choice_steps 2 <=4, attributes 5 <=20, hidden_elements 1 <=3, random_draws 0 <=1, rounds 6 <=15. However Step1 Poisoner chooses L/R, Step2 Chooser chooses L/R without seeing Step1 is a choice made at same moment by several players described as sequential turns. Engine requires simultaneous_choice as a simultaneous poll, not sequential hidden turns. As written it violates the flat simultaneous-poll requirement and would need workaround to implement correctly.
- reasoning_over_luck FAILED: No external random_draws; winner is sum of match/mismatch points. What to infer: only opponent's current L/R. Intended good strategy is Nash 50/50 mixing to be unexploitable. If both follow it, each round is independent Bernoulli p=0.5, total ~ Binomial(6,0.5). Winner is then decided by players' own randomization noise: P(win)=22/64, P(draw)=20/64, P(loss)=22/64 for either player. Chance alone decides winner/draw ~100% of variance; skill cannot improve win rate against optimal opponent. Against non-optimal opponent there is something to exploit (detect bias over 6-round history), but against optimal opponent there is nothing to infer and optimal play randomizes, so reasoning is not clearly favoured: Always-L vs Nash also gets 34.4% win vs Nash, same as Nash vs Nash. No private type, no payoff asymmetry, no memory beyond 6 one-shot matches, so opponent modelling has negligible edge within 6 rounds and luck dominates.
- change: Playable: Merge Step1 and Step2 into one simultaneous_choice poll where both players simultaneously declare L/R, then reveal together; renumber steps to 1-poll, 2-reveal, 3-score, and update designer's steps_per_round/choice_steps accordingly (e.g., steps_per_round 3, choice_steps 1).
- change: Reasoning: Add inferable structure so optimal play is not pure 50/50 coin flips. Examples: give Poisoner a persistent private bias/type for the match, or make payoff asymmetric, or add cheap talk/bluff channel, or carryover signal (e.g., Poisoner poison costs or Chooser gets bonus for correct streak), or increase rounds to 10-15 and score prediction accuracy, so history and opponent modelling materially raise win rate above chance; ensure that two Nash players do not have winner decided by randomization alone.
The variation that was reviewed: Battle of Wits - Six Goblets: Players: 2 (Player A, Player B). Rounds: 6 fixed. Roles alternate: Round 1,3,5 Player A is Poisoner and Player B is Chooser; Round 2,4,6 Player B is Poisoner and Player A is Chooser. Each round: Step 1 - Poisoner secretly chooses a goblet to poison: L or R (exactly one). Step 2 - Chooser, without seeing Step 1, chooses a goblet to drink: L or R. Step 3 - Reveal: both choices are announced. Step 4 - Scoring: compute match = 1 if Poisoner choice == Chooser choice else 0. Poisoner gains match points (1 if match else 0). Chooser gains 1-match points (1 if mismatch else 0). Exactly 1 point awarded per round, 6 points total. No other actions, no communication, no immunity, no both-goblets option. Scores start at 0 and accumulate. Winner: After 6 rounds, any player with total >=4 wins. If exactly one player reaches >=4, that player wins. If both reach >=4 (4-4 or higher tie impossible with 6 points max 6 but 3-3 is possible -> no one reaches 4 => draw) the result is a draw. If neither reaches 4 (e.g., 3-3) the result is a draw. Winner is decided solely by arithmetic comparison to target 4, not by highest total alone.
