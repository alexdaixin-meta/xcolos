# Reasoning and planning: Ripening Hats

**Verdict: strong.** Winning rewards inferring rivals' favourites from the anonymous outcomes and timing one snipe so that your own colour ripens meanwhile. In practice, most players settled into favourite lanes and the results were decided by one or two well-timed grabs. Fewer rounds, a higher favourite value or a stronger penalty for idling would make the timing and prediction the designer describes matter more often.

From 3 play(s): 288 choices, 215 with a reason given (about 30.1 words each); results {'seat 3': 1, 'seats 2 and 4': 1, 'seats 3 and 4': 1}.

## Reasoning: 2 of 3

Most choice turns weigh the favourite's sure 1+3 against a riper colour's risk of a clash, and several players draw real inferences from the anonymous results: who lied, who the rival for a colour is, and rough score estimates. Many turns are still formulaic ('favourite pays 4, safer'), and a recurring error was each player assuming the others earn only 1 a round when all were sitting on their favourites.

- match 2, turn 15, seat 3: "Seats 1 and 2 most likely took Yellow and Green, as they had announced. That leaves seat 4 as the other Purple chooser, despite saying Red." (Works out which hidden player caused a clash from the unattributed result.)
- match 2, turn 14, seat 2: "Green pays them only 1, and they already lied in round 1. They said Red, yet Red went untouched." (Uses the announced outcome to expose a bluff and discount a claim.)
- match 2, turn 59, seat 3: "Blue has clashed twice, and both times Yellow went untouched, so the Yellow player is probably my rival for Blue." (Reads UNTOUCHED patterns to identify the opponent in a contest.)
- match 2, turn 94, seat 2: "Seat 4 most likely has about 40 points from collecting Red, their likely favourite, every round since round 2, and I have 42." (Estimates a rival's hidden favourite and score correctly and picks the move that wins.)

## Planning: 2 of 3

Several players time a grab so that their own favourite ripens while they take another colour, then come back to it. Seat 2 in match 1 carried this out over two rounds, though other stated plans (match 3 seat 2's Green grab) were dropped. Most turns are still chosen for the current round only, and lane-keeping is passive rather than planned.

- match 1, turn 86, seat 2: "If I take Blue now, Red keeps ripening to 3, and I can take Red in the last round for 3 + 3 = 6." (Two-round plan that was carried out (Blue collected in round 11, Red taken in round 12).)
- match 3, turn 87, seat 3: "Green pays 5 now. Blue then sits untouched, rises to 2, and pays 2 + 3 = 5 in round 12. That totals 10, against 8 for staying on Blue both rounds." (Ripening plan carried out, and it won the match.)
- match 1, turn 31, seat 3: "Grabbing now, while everyone still says "leave Blue", is less crowded than waiting until it reaches 7." (Times a snipe around what others are expected to do later.)

## Luck: 1 of 3

The only chance element is the deal of favourites, which does not change values. Collisions came from simultaneous choices that players reasoned about. Margins of 1 to 2 points meant that a coin-flip-like guess on a single contest could swing the result.

- match 2, turn 39, seat 3: "It only pays if no one else grabs Blue too. I think the chance of that is under half" (Contests are treated as guesses about others, not as dice.)

## Weak spots

- match 1, turn 80, seat 4: Used the same 'round 10 is less expected than the final round' logic as seat 2 and collided on Blue. This is the copycat trap the designer warned about; staying on Yellow and taking Blue in round 11 was better reasoned.
- match 2, turn 78, seat 2: As the leader, left safe Green for Blue, which seat 1 had repeatedly chased, and clashed. Seat 3's announced block of Green was cheap talk that seat 3 did not carry out. Staying on Green was the better-reasoned option.
- match 3, turn 86, seat 2: Had concluded at turn 82 that it needed ripe Green to pass seat 1, then reverted to Purple on weak grounds and finished 48, behind seats 3 and 4 on 51.
- match 3, turn 93, seat 1: Believed 'I'm far ahead at 45' because it assumed others score 1 a round, though every seat had visibly been collecting its own favourite. It never sought upside and lost.
