Original game: Jukha
Players in the original: 3 to 13

Original rules:
Use 4 cards of each rank per player plus one Joker in total. Deal all cards clockwise so player left of dealer gets 5, others 4; keep hands hidden. Suits irrelevant. On a turn, if you have 4 cards and they are all equal, show them and exit and cannot lose. Otherwise you must request a card from the player who currently has 5 cards. You may name a rank you want. The 5-card player must offer one card face down and may claim any rank, truthfully or not. You may take it and end your turn with 5 cards, or reject it to ask for a second. If second offered, you must take one of the two offered and other returns. You may instead ask for a third; then you must take the third and both first return. After your turn you have 5 and are asked next. Having 4 of a kind plus extra does not let you exit; you need exactly 4 at start of your turn. Joker cannot exit. Play until only two players remain; they both lose.

A PREVIOUS VARIATION of this game was reviewed and tested. The findings:
- playable FAILED: Complexity is within limits: 11 steps of at most 20, 3 choice steps of at most 4, 16 attributes of at most 20, 1 hidden element of at most 3, 1 random draw per round (the blind draw; the shuffle is setup only), 30 rounds of at most 40. No board, clock, judge, trading or binding is needed. Scoring is arithmetic on recorded hands. Leaving out free talk is sensible. The only one who can block a near-winner is the taker, who already wants to block, so talk would mostly add words. Requests and claims already supply the bluff channel. Defect: the turn-counter rule contradicts itself. 'Turns are numbered from 1' implies the counter starts at 1. Step 6 then reads 'add 1; if the counter has reached 30, go to FALLBACK', which triggers after turn 29. But FALLBACK says 'only after 30 turns' and scores 'the taker of turn 30'. The counter's starting value is never stated. One implementer will end after 29 turns and another after 30. That changes who holds 5 cards at the end and therefore the fallback result. A model working only from these words cannot settle this.

Each player knows only their own hand, the cards they have personally given or received, and the public announcements listed below.

TURN ORDER. Each turn has a giver, who holds 5 cards, and a taker, the next seat in order, who holds 4. On turn 1 the giver is the first giver. After each turn, that turn's taker becomes the next giver, and the following seat becomes the next taker.

EACH TURN (turns are numbered from 1):
1. Exit check. If the taker's 4 cards are all the same rank (four A, four B or four C), the taker shows them and wins at once, and the other two players lose. The Joker never counts toward a set.
2. Request (public). The taker names one rank they want (A, B or C) or says 'none'. The request binds no one.
3. Offer (public claims). The giver chooses two distinct physical cards from their hand; they may be the same rank. The giver places them face down as Offer 1 and Offer 2 and publicly claims a rank for each (A, B, C or Joker). Claims may be true or false.
4. Take (public choice). The taker announces one of three options: 'Offer 1', 'Offer 2' or 'blind'. Blind means receiving one card chosen uniformly at random from the giver's three unoffered cards. The choice is announced to all players. Only the taker sees the received card. The giver knows which card left their hand. The third player learns only the announced choice. Cards not taken stay in the giver's hand unrevealed.
5. Pass. The received card moves from the giver's hand to the taker's hand. Record this card privately as the taker's 'last received' card. The taker now holds 5 cards and the giver holds 4.
6. Advance. Add 1 to the turn counter. If the counter has reached 30, go to FALLBACK. Otherwise begin the next turn.

FALLBACK (only after 30 turns with no exit). All hands are revealed, and each player's score is computed as follows.
- Group count. This is the largest number of cards of one rank (A, B or C) among the scored cards, at most 4. For the two players holding 4 cards, the scored cards are their whole hand. For the player holding 5 cards (the taker of turn 30), the scored cards are their 5 cards minus their 'last received' card, that is, the 4 cards they held at turn 30's exit check.
- Joker penalty. The player who physically holds the Joker subtracts 2, whether or not it was their last received card.
- Outcome. If exactly one player has the highest score, that player wins and the other two lose. If two or three players tie for the highest score, the whole game is a draw.

There is no free talk. The only public statements are requests, claims and the taker's choice. Winner: The first player who begins their taking turn holding exactly four cards of one rank (A, B or C) wins, and the other two lose. If no one does so in 30 turns, scores are computed: largest same-rank group (at most 4) among the scored cards, where the 5-card holder excludes the card received on turn 30, minus 2 for whoever holds the Joker. A single top scorer wins and the others lose. Any tie for top is a draw for the whole game.

Revise the variation to fix these. Keep what works. Review it again against the criteria.
