# Reasoning and planning: Gracias: The Counted Gift

**Verdict: adequate.** Winning rewards tracking public counts and placing gifts to force majority discards or deny stacks, and players did this in most decisions. Margins were thin and face-down cards often decided majorities. Fewer hidden cards, or a scoring bonus that rewards counting rivals' hidden cards, would make deep inference and plans over several turns matter more.

From 3 play(s): 144 choices, 144 with a reason given (about 77.4 words each); results {'seat 4': 1, 'seat 2': 2}.

## Reasoning: 2 of 3

Most decisions weigh keeps and gifts against public counts. Players aim to push a rival's loose count to the discard majority, avoid handing over a fourth card that completes a stack, and sometimes count unseen cards to judge face-down odds. Inference about rivals' hidden cards is rare and sometimes wrong, and several gifts fed a rival the third or fourth card of a colour they were building, so the play falls short of consistently correct inference.

- match 1, turn 30, seat 1: "The cards I can't see are 2 Red, 4 Blue, 1 Green and 6 Yellow, so T3's face-down card is likely Yellow or Blue." (Counts the unseen cards, as the designer intended, to choose between two triplets.)
- match 2, turn 47, seat 2: "It can't be discarded, because seat 1 already shows 6 Yellow (at least loose 2). Only one unseen Yellow is left, so no one else can pass that." (Bounds what others can reach from the remaining cards and judges the loose point safe.)
- match 1, turn 16, seat 3: "raises seat 1 from 2 to 3 yellow. That ties my 3 yellow for the highest loose value." (Uses a gift on purpose to force a tied majority discard on a rival.)
- match 2, turn 39, seat 4: "A fourth makes a stack, so seat 1 gains 3 points but has no loose Yellow left." (Second-order reasoning: completes a weak player's stack so that the leader becomes the Yellow majority.)

## Planning: 2 of 3

Players keep colours toward stacks across rounds and steer gifts by game standings and tiebreaks. A few anticipate the picks still to come in the round. Plans across several turns that depend on opponents are rare, and most look-ahead covers only the current round or session.

- match 1, turn 42, seat 4: "If we tie on total, I win on best session score (11 vs 9)." (Plans the final session around the tiebreak against the leader.)
- match 2, turn 23, seat 4: "Seat 1 will take the Red-Red triplet last, so another Red could be gifted to me." (Predicts a later pick in the round and keeps a colour to exploit it.)
- match 1, turn 14, seat 1: "Giving it to seat 3 would put them at 3 Green while they still have a pick, so they could reach a full stack of 4." (Accounts for an opponent's remaining pick this round when choosing the gift receiver.)

## Luck: 2 of 3

A quarter of the cards are face-down and unknown, and they often decide whether a majority falls where a player predicted or whether a stack completes. Results were close (22 vs 20, 24 vs 23, 21 vs 20), so the hidden draws plausibly decided the winners.

- match 1, turn 30, seat 1: "If the hidden card is Yellow (6 of 13), I reach 4, a stack worth 3 points." (Stack completion hinges on a face-down draw.)
- match 3, turn 16, seat 3: "The exception is if this round's face-down card is Red, which would give me a full stack of 4." (The outcome rests on an unseen card the player cannot control.)

## Weak spots

- match 1, turn 21, seat 2: Gifted a third Red to seat 4, who already showed 2 Red, assuming it would be discarded. Seat 4 completed a Red stack two picks later. Giving the Red to seat 1 or seat 3, who showed no Red, was the safer gift.
- match 1, turn 38, seat 3: Gave seat 1 its fourth public Red, completing a 3-point stack, while chasing a 'give to the trailer' policy. Seat 1 finished only 2 points behind the winner. Seat 4 had flagged this same risk two picks earlier.
- match 1, turn 7, seat 4: Gifted Blue to seat 1, who already had Blue, to try to create a majority discard. That helped seat 1 build to a Blue stack. A seat with no Blue was the better receiver.
