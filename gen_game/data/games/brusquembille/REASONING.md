# Reasoning and planning: Mirror Brusquembille

**Verdict: adequate.** Winning rewards card counting, trump-exposure odds and planning the follow-suit endgame, and the players mostly did these correctly, but draw luck decided some games. Making more of the deal deducible earlier, or giving each player a choice in what they draw, would shift weight from the pile order to planning.

From 3 play(s): 89 choices, 89 with a reason given (about 65.7 words each); results {'seat 2': 1, 'seat 1': 2}.

## Reasoning: 2 of 3

From the middle game on, players count the opponent's remaining hand-plus-pile cards correctly, quote the odds of a trump in the opponent's hand, and weigh leads against those odds. Many early decisions are only 'lead the zero-point card' or 'discard the cheapest card', which is sound but generic.

- match 1, turn 19, seat 1: "the opponent's last 7 unseen cards are KS, TH, KH, AD, KD, AC and KC, and 3 of those are in their hand... The chance they hold one is 1 − C(5,3)/C(7,3) ≈ 71%" (An exact card count plus a correct combinatorial probability, used to compare leads.)
- match 2, turn 5, seat 1: "The opponent threw away KH and TH rather than trumping, which suggests they may hold few or no diamonds." (Infers the hidden hand from the opponent's discards.)
- match 1, turn 13, seat 2: "their remaining 10 cards are AH, TH, AD, TD, KD, 7D, AC, TC, 7C and TS. To win this trick they would have to use TS or a trump" (A correct full count of the opponent's remaining cards guides a safe lead.)

## Planning: 2 of 3

Players often keep Aces and top trumps for the must-follow tricks 14–16. Several multi-trick endgame plans that depend on the opponent's forced replies were stated and then carried out (match 2 seat 1 from turn 26), but most middle-game moves are still chosen trick by trick.

- match 2, turn 26, seat 1: "If they lead TS, my AS wins it. I then lead TC, which beats either of their clubs. I lead 7S last and win" (A full plan for tricks 14–16 that relies on follow-suit, later carried out at turns 28–29.)
- match 3, turn 9, seat 2: "From trick 14 on, the follower must follow suit, so an Ace led then almost always wins. That makes the Aces most valuable late in the game." (Keeps resources for the endgame phase.)
- match 1, turn 24, seat 1: "I hold TD and TS. I can win every remaining trick if they lead KS or KD at trick 13." (Chooses a discard by planning around the opponent's possible next leads.)

## Luck: 2 of 3

Which cards each player drew, especially trumps and Aces, decided several outcomes. Match 3 was settled 101–100 on whether one Ace was in hand or still in the pile, and players themselves said when the draws had already decided the result.

- match 3, turn 25, seat 2: "this one wins whenever their Ace of Clubs (AC) is still in their pile, about a 1-in-4 chance" (A one-point game decided by the hidden pile order.)
- match 2, turn 25, seat 2: "Opponent can hold Ace of Spades and Ten of Clubs. With good play they also win my Ten of Spades, so my position is probably lost." (The card distribution left no winning choice.)

## Weak spots

- match 2, turn 21, seat 2: Led AH knowing that the opponent's only trump, TD, was among their six cards (three of them in hand). This handed over 21 points. A cheaper probe such as KC, keeping AH for a must-follow trick, was the better-reasoned option.
- match 3, turn 21, seat 1: Miscounted the trumps in its own words: 'They hold the last four trumps (AD, TD)', when only two remained. The lead still worked, but the stated count was wrong.
