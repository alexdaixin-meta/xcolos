# Reasoning and planning: Blind Officers' Schafkopf

**Verdict: adequate.** Winning rewards real reasoning (void and Blind inference, card counting) and match-score planning in bids and doubles, as the designer intended. The deal still dominates and decisive results came from the double choice, so a longer match, more bidding information or a smaller Blind would make skill matter more.

From 3 play(s): 181 choices, 181 with a reason given (about 53.9 words each); results {'seat 1': 3}.

## Reasoning: 2 of 3

Most free decisions rest on specific inference: voids deduced from discards, unseen cards counted, cards placed in the Blind, and the bid used to place the opponent's suits. Many moves are forced follows explained only by restating the rules, and in eight double decisions the stated reasoning (No double) contradicts the action taken (Double).

- match 1, turn 54, seat 1: "my Leaves Unter drew a Leaves Nine. That means seat 2 holds no trumps. The other two Unters must be in the Blind, so my Hearts Unter is the only trump left in play." (Correctly infers from one card that the remaining trumps sit in the hidden Blind, which is the designer's central inference.)
- match 1, turn 39, seat 2: "On trick 3 they gave up the Bells Ace (11 points) under my Bells Ober. If they had a higher Ober they would have won the trick, and with a cheap Unter or the Bells Nine they would have played that instead." (Reads the opponent's trump holding from what a rational opponent would have played.)
- match 1, turn 15, seat 2: "Keeping Leaves Ten covers two cards instead of one, so I give up Acorns Ten now." (Works out endgame odds by counting which unseen last cards each Ten would beat.)
- match 3, turn 51, seat 1: "My opponent played Hearts Unter under my Hearts Ober instead of winning the trick, so they probably hold neither Acorns Ober nor Leaves Ober." (Infers the missing high trumps from the defender's underplay.)
- match 2, turn 25, seat 2: "I keep Hearts Nine so that if Hearts Ace is led, I can follow with the Nine and save my Hearts Ten." (Chooses a discard by weighing likely future leads.)

## Planning: 2 of 3

Players plan bids and doubles around the four-hand match score: leaders pass, trailers bid Strong, and players track what the opponent can still reach. Within hands they create voids, save trumps for later ruffs and order their sure winners, though plans rarely run beyond a few tricks.

- match 1, turn 4, seat 1: "I'll lose my worthless Nines first to make myself void in suits. Then whatever they lead outside Bells, I can trump it and take the points." (A multi-trick plan to set up ruffs, carried out in tricks 3, 6, 7 and 8.)
- match 2, turn 30, seat 1: "To win the match they would need to make a Strong contract in both hands (4 + 4)." (A two-hand look-ahead on the opponent's paths to victory drives a safe pass.)
- match 1, turn 48, seat 2: "I choose No double. If they make it, the match is drawn. If they fail, I win." (Plans the bid and the double together, based on the opponent's likely bid.)
- match 3, turn 46, seat 1: "I'm behind 4 to 8, so a Simple win (at most +4) or Ramsch (+1) can't win the match. Only a Strong contract gives me a chance." (Chooses the bid to fit what the match score requires, as the designer intended.)

## Luck: 2 of 3

The deal largely decided who could declare and whether contracts were made. Several hands were won by holding all four Unters or long trumps, and two matches turned on a doubled Strong contract made with a favourable deal.

- match 1, turn 1, seat 1: "I hold all four Unters, so in Wenz the opponent has no trumps at all." (The deal handed seat 1 total trump control.)
- match 3, turn 14, seat 2: "So it's a coin flip. I'll keep Leaves Ten and give up Acorns Ten." (The hand's result hinged on an unknowable last card.)
