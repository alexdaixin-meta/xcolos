# Reasoning and planning: Coloretto: Secret Favourites

**Verdict: adequate.** Winning rewards careful score arithmetic and some reading of rivals' likely takes, and the best results came from predicting a rival's reply. Inferring Favourites was weak and card order swings the small margins; longer games, or scoring that punishes a revealed Favourite more, would make inference and multi-cycle planning matter more.

From 3 play(s): 73 choices, 73 with a reason given (about 56.5 words each); results {'seat 2': 1, 'seat 3': 2}.

## Reasoning: 2 of 3

Most non-forced decisions compute exact score changes, and several weigh what rivals will do next or how many cards remain, such as the remaining Greens and Purples or the unseen Blues. Inference of rivals' Favourites is shallow and sometimes wrong, and some placements rest on loose 'less tempting' judgements, so most decisions fall short of specific, correct inference.

- match 3, turn 20, seat 3: "Yellow 2 ties Purple 2 and only one of them counts positive, so my score drops to 3... Only about 3 Greens remain in roughly 21 cards." (Correct use of the tie rule and a card count to compare options.)
- match 3, turn 23, seat 1: "I would reach 6. But seat 2 would then take the +2 row and reach about 8, so I would lose." (Judges the option by the rival's reply, not only by its own score.)
- match 2, turn 24, seat 3: "I hold Red (my Favourite) 2 and Blue 3, which scores 9 right now. Taking a row with one Red raises Red to 3, for 6 + 6 = 12." (Accurate scoring arithmetic plus a judgement of the rivals' standing.)
- match 1, turn 22, seat 2: "Seat 1 (3 Purple, likely their Favourite) could then take row 1 and tie me." (Tries to infer a Favourite from takes, as the designer intended, though the inference was wrong (seat 1's Favourite was Blue).)

## Planning: 2 of 3

Players regularly build rows to take later and set up rows as bait. A few multi-step plans that rely on a rival's reply were made and paid off, such as M1 t22 and M3 t22, but planning across cycles is mostly limited to 'keep the tableau clean'.

- match 1, turn 22, seat 2: "seat 1's best reply is to take row 2 (one Green) for about 7. I then take row 1 and reach Red 3, Green 1, Yellow 1, +2 1, which scores 8." (A two-step plan that depends on the rival's reply; it was carried out and won the match.)
- match 3, turn 22, seat 2: "Seat 1 deliberately built the Yellow pair in row 2 and will probably take it. That would leave me the +2 row" (Sets up a later take by predicting the rival's take.)
- match 2, turn 15, seat 3: "builds a row worth about +4 to me... Taking row 3 now would give only +3" (Delays taking in order to build a better row for later.)

## Luck: 2 of 3

Final margins were 2 to 5 points, and the order of cards off the deck often decided who got a clean row. Several plans succeeded or failed on the next card turned up.

- match 3, turn 21, seat 1: "If they add a card to that row instead, I still come out ahead: a Purple would lift me to 9" (Outcomes hinge on the next card drawn.)
- match 1, turn 22, seat 2: "The risk is that the next card is Purple and seat 1 builds Green plus Purple in row 2" (The winning plan was exposed to the next draw.)

## Weak spots

- match 3, turn 19, seat 2: Claimed that adding Purple to the Red row makes it 'less tempting' to the two Purple holders. It made the row more attractive, and seat 3 took it to win. Putting the Purple with the Yellow, or in row 3, would have denied it.
- match 1, turn 4, seat 1: Placed their own Favourite, Blue, next to a Purple in a row that seat 3 could take, and seat 3 took it at once. Seat 1 ended with 0 Blue. Placing Blue in row 3 with the +2 and taking that row was the better-reasoned line.
- match 1, turn 13, seat 3: Built a two-Purple row while noting that seat 1 held Purple and might take it, which seat 1 then did. Taking the single Purple, or placing the card elsewhere, would have protected the Favourite.
