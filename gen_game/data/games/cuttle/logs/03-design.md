Original game: Cuttle
Players in the original: 2 to 2

Original rules:
Two players share a standard 52-card deck. Deal 6 cards to dealer and 5 to opponent, rest is draw pile; scrap pile is face-up discard showing top only. On your turn do exactly one: draw one card, or play one card from hand. Number cards A,2-10 can be point cards worth their value (A=1) placed on your side; first to 21+ points wins, but kings reduce requirement to 14/10/7/5 for 1-4 kings you control. A point card can also scuttle: play a higher rank, or equal rank with higher suit C<D<H<S, to discard both cards. Cards  A,2-7,9 can be one-offs discarded after use for effects like wipe points, destroy permanents, recover from scrap, force opponent discard 2, draw 2, play top of deck immediately, bounce a permanent. 8,J,Q,K are permanents staying on table: 8 forces opponent to reveal hand, J stacked on a point card steals it, Q protects your other points/permanents from single-target effects, K lowers win threshold. 2 can be played on opponent's turn to counter a one-off. If draw pile empty you may pass; three passes ends game with no winner.

A PREVIOUS VARIATION of this game was reviewed and tested. The findings:
- balanced FAILED: THOUGHTLESS POLICIES. (1) Always DRAW, the first option: it never scores. Against any opponent who plays points it loses, so it fails as a strategy, which is fine. (2) Always PASS: illegal while you can draw, so it counts as PASS anyway and loses, which is fine. (3) Greedy G: play a King if held, else the highest point card, else draw. This is the real problem. It is close to the optimal line, not a thoughtless one that sensible play beats.

RACE ARITHMETIC. 40 of 48 cards are point cards, mean rank 5.5. The top three of a 5-card hand are about 8+7+5 = 20, so a typical hand reaches 21 in 3 plays. One King cuts that to 14, i.e. 2 plays.
- Jacks only steal rank 7 or less. G plays its 8s to 10s first, so a Jack rarely has a target against G.
- Scuttle is the only defence. To scuttle a 9 you need a 9 or 10. P(at least one of the 8 such cards in 6 cards) = 1 - C(40,6)/C(48,6) = 1 - 0.313 = 0.687. To scuttle a 10 you need a 10: 1 - C(44,6)/C(48,6) = 1 - 0.575 = 0.425.
- Kings cannot be answered at all: no Queen, no destroy effect, no scuttle of a King. The first King is worth 7 permanent, untouchable points, so a King should always be played at once.

SEAT EDGE, by move order. First plays a high card on turn 1.
- If Second races, First makes its 3rd play in round 3 before Second's 3rd play, so First wins.
- If Second scuttles, both have spent one card and one turn. Progress is back to 0-0 and it is First's turn again. First keeps the initiative and Second must answer every play until it runs out of matching high cards.
- Second's extra 6th card buys roughly one extra scuttle. First's Kings cannot be scuttled.
- My estimate is that First wins about 60-65% between equal greedy players.

WINNER RULE. Against sensible or random play, G as First almost never loses unless the opponent was dealt Kings or several 10s. Neither seat has a real choice beyond G plus 'scuttle if you can'. No option is strictly dominant in every state, but the King play is dominant and the first seat has a structural edge.
- reasoning_over_luck FAILED: The game usually ends by rounds 3-6, so the deal decides most outcomes.
- Who holds Kings (4 of 48 cards, unanswerable, worth 7/4/3/2 threshold points) and who holds 10s (the only answer to a 10) mostly decides the winner.
- P(First dealt at least 1 King in 5 cards) = 1 - C(44,5)/C(48,5) = 0.36. P(Second, 6 cards) = 0.43.

With both players on the intended good line (King at once, high cards, scuttle when able), the result follows from the deal plus the seat edge. My estimate is that 70% or more of results are fixed by the deal and seat.

Inference is thin. You can count public scrap to judge whether the opponent can still scuttle a 10, and decide whether to hold a card back. But there is almost no reason to bluff or hold back, because a delay only gives the opponent tempo. A better reasoner is not clearly favoured.

ROUND: Each round the First player takes a turn, then the Second player takes a turn. At the start of each turn the engine shows everyone the public state: both point piles, both point totals, both King counts and thresholds, the scrap pile, the size of the draw pile, and both hand sizes. Only the acting player sees their own hand.

TURN: The acting player chooses exactly one action.
(a) DRAW: Take the top card of the draw pile into your hand. This is not allowed if your hand already holds 8 cards or the draw pile is empty.
(b) POINTS: Put one point card from your hand into your point pile.
(c) SCUTTLE: Choose one point card from your hand and one card in the opponent's point pile whose rank is equal to or lower than it. Both cards go to the scrap pile.
(d) JACK: Play a Jack from your hand and choose one card of rank 7 or less in the opponent's point pile. That card moves to your point pile, and the Jack goes to the scrap pile.
(e) KING: Put a King from your hand on the table. Your King count rises by 1 and your threshold falls accordingly.
(f) PASS: Allowed only if you cannot draw, either because the draw pile is empty or because you hold 8 cards. A player with 8 cards who holds a point card should play instead; passing is allowed but wastes the turn.
An illegal or unparseable choice counts as PASS.

WIN CHECK: Immediately after every turn, if either player's points are at or above their threshold, that player wins at once. A steal can push the acting player over; only the acting player's action can change totals, so at most one player can cross on a turn.

END: The game stops after round 30. If nobody has won by then, the player with the smaller shortfall wins. Equal shortfalls are a draw. Winner: You win immediately if, after any turn, your point total (the sum of the ranks in your point pile) is at least your threshold (21, 14, 10, 7 or 5 for 0 to 4 Kings played). If nobody has done so after 30 rounds, the player with the smaller shortfall (threshold minus points, minimum 0) wins. Equal shortfalls are a draw.
