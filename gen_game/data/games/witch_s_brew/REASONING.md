## Reasoning: 2 of 3

Many decisions are only the positional rule ('I sit last, so contest'), but a real share rebuild hands from earlier reveals, attach rough odds to who holds a role, and check final-round score bounds. Some of that inference was wrong, including reading a leader's 'none' as proof of a bluff and taking a message as proof someone lacked a role.

- match 1, turn 55, seat 2: "they led the Raven Keeper and showed they have no Thief, so the Witch is the likely third role. Contesting only pays off if seat 3 lacks the Witch, which I put at about 30% (5 × 0.3 = 1.5). That is worse than a sure 2." (Rebuilds an opponent's hand from what has been revealed and compares the expected value with the safe yield.)
- match 3, turn 59, seat 1: "It responded to the Witch and the Thief and took the Druid as leader, so its hand is full." (Correctly deduces that one opponent's hand is spent, which narrows who can contest.)
- match 2, turn 20, seat 4: "Seat 1 passed on the Druid, so they hold 3 of the other 5 roles, which is about a 60% chance they hold the Witch." (Turns a role an opponent did not respond to into a probability estimate for the next contest.)
- match 1, turn 86, seat 2: "If I name the Herb Hag, which I don't hold, the most anyone can reach is 11 + 2 = 13. That is below my 15 whatever they do, so I win." (Bounds every opponent's best outcome to pick the lead that locks the win.)

## Planning: 2 of 3

Players often keep high-value roles for the round where they sit last, and pick hands with the coming leader rotation in mind. Several of these plans were carried out over two or three rounds, one of them depending on another player's predicted lead.

- match 2, turn 54, seat 4: "I'm keeping my Raven Keeper... If seat 1 names it next round, I sit last and can't be overtaken." (Holds a role for a later favourable position; seat 4 took the 6 at turn 65 as planned.)
- match 2, turn 82, seat 1: "If I name the Raven Keeper now, I keep the Witch for the last round. The Witch is the role seat 2 is more likely to name there." (A plan resting on another player's likely lead; seat 2 did name the Witch and seat 1 contested it from the last seat.)
- match 1, turn 5, seat 1: "In every later round this set I sit last in clockwise order, so my contests can't be overtaken. I'll save Witch and Raven Keeper for those rounds." (Spends the weakest role from the weak leading seat and keeps strong roles for later; seat 1 then won the Witch.)

## Luck: 1 of 3

There is no random device. Uncertainty comes only from opponents' simultaneous hand choices and their claims, so outcomes turn on guessing other players. Overlapping hands added some variance at the margin, such as who happened to hold the Raven Keeper when sitting last.

- match 3, turn 65, seat 3: "Seat 4 has already used all three of its roles this set (Witch, Thief, Druid), so nobody can overtake me." (Outcomes follow from hand states that can be deduced, not from chance.)
